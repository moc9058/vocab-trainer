# 1. Definitions

## 1.1. Transform and inverse (§6.1)

For a causal function f(t), define its Laplace transform by

$$
F(s)=\mathcal L\{f(t)\}=\int_0^\infty e^{-st}f(t)\,dt.
$$

The inverse operation is denoted by $\mathcal L^{-1}\{F(s)\}=f(t)$. A function is of exponential order k if it is eventually bounded by a constant times $e^{kt}$.

## 1.2. Step, impulse, and convolution (§§6.3–6.5)

The unit step $u(t-a)$ equals zero before time a and one afterward. The Dirac impulse $\delta(t-a)$ is a generalized function with sampling property. The convolution of causal functions is

$$
(f*g)(t)=\int_0^t f(\tau)g(t-\tau)\,d\tau.
$$

The transformed ODE's algebraic equation for $Y(s)$ is called the subsidiary equation.

# 2. Theorems

## 2.1. Linearity and existence (§6.1)

The transform is linear: $\mathcal L\{af+bg\}=aF+bG$. If f is piecewise continuous on finite intervals and of exponential order k, its transform exists for real $s>k$.

## 2.2. First shifting theorem (§6.1)

$$
\mathcal L\{e^{at}f(t)\}=F(s-a).
$$

## 2.3. Transforms of derivatives and integrals (§6.2)

$$
\mathcal L\{f'\}=sF(s)-f(0).
$$

For integer n at least 1,

$$
\mathcal L\{f^{(n)}\}=s^nF(s)-s^{n-1}f(0)-\cdots-f^{(n-1)}(0).
$$

Also,

$$
\mathcal L\left\{\int_0^t f(\tau)\,d\tau\right\}=F(s)/s.
$$

## 2.4. Second shifting theorem (§6.3)

For a positive delay a,

$$
\mathcal L\{f(t-a)u(t-a)\}=e^{-as}F(s).
$$

## 2.5. Convolution theorem (§6.5)

$$
\mathcal L\{f*g\}=F(s)G(s).
$$

## 2.6. Differentiation of transforms (§6.6)

$$
\mathcal L\{tf(t)\}=-F'(s),\qquad \mathcal L\{t^nf(t)\}=(-1)^nF^{(n)}(s).
$$

# 3. Solution Methods and Fundamental Formulas

## 3.1. Standard transforms (§§6.1, 6.8–6.9)

$$
\mathcal L\{1\}=1/s,\quad \mathcal L\{t^n\}=n!/s^{n+1},\quad \mathcal L\{e^{at}\}=1/(s-a).
$$

$$
\mathcal L\{\sin\omega t\}=\omega/(s^2+\omega^2),\quad
\mathcal L\{\cos\omega t\}=s/(s^2+\omega^2).
$$

## 3.2. Linear initial-value problems (§6.2)

Transform the ODE, substitute initial conditions, solve the subsidiary equation for Y(s), and invert. For $y''+ay'+by=r(t)$ with $y(0)=y_0$ and $y'(0)=v_0$,

$$
(s^2+as+b)Y(s)=R(s)+sy_0+v_0+ay_0.
$$

## 3.3. Piecewise inputs (§6.3)

Express switched forcing using $u(t-a)$ and the second shifting theorem. In particular $\mathcal L\{u(t-a)\}=e^{-as}/s$.

## 3.4. Impulses and partial fractions (§6.4)

$$
\mathcal L\{\delta(t-a)\}=e^{-as}.
$$

An ideal impulse models a short concentrated input. To invert rational transforms, factor denominators and apply partial fractions, including repeated roots and irreducible quadratic factors.

## 3.5. Integral equations (§6.5)

For $y=f+k*y$, transforming gives $Y=F+KY$ and hence $Y=F/(1-K)$ wherever defined.

## 3.6. Variable-coefficient ODEs (§6.6)

Differentiation in s handles time factors. For example,

$$
\mathcal L\{ty'(t)\}=-\frac{d}{ds}[sY(s)-y(0)].
$$

## 3.7. Systems of ODEs (§6.7)

For $\mathbf y'=A\mathbf y+\mathbf g(t)$ with constant matrix A and initial state $\mathbf y_0$,

$$
(sI-A)\mathbf Y(s)=\mathbf y_0+\mathbf G(s).
$$

Solve the matrix algebraic equation and invert componentwise.

# 4. Applications and Connections

- Mechanical oscillations: $my''+cy'+ky=F(t)$; Laplace transforms incorporate initial displacement and velocity and accommodate switched or impulsive forcing.
- Electrical circuits: transform circuit ODEs with initial charge/current, voltage steps, and pulses.
- Linear time-invariant response: if $Y(s)=H(s)F(s)$ with zero initial state, then $y=h*f$, where $h$ is the inverse transform of H.
- Sections 6.8–6.9 collect operational identities and transform pairs.
