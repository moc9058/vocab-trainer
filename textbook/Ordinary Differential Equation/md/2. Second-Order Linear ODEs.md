# 1. Definitions

## 1.1. Second-order linear ODE (Sec. 2.1)

- In standard form, a second-order linear ODE is $y''+p(x)y'+q(x)y=r(x)$.
- The equation is **homogeneous** if $r(x)\equiv0$; otherwise it is **nonhomogeneous**.
- A solution on an open interval $I$ is a twice-differentiable function satisfying the equation throughout $I$.

## 1.2. Initial value problem (Sec. 2.1)

- A second-order IVP prescribes both the function value and its first derivative at one point:

$$
y''+py'+qy=r,\qquad y(x_0)=K_0,\quad y'(x_0)=K_1.
$$

## 1.3. Linear independence and basis (Secs. 2.1, 2.6)

- Functions $y_1,y_2$ are linearly independent on $I$ if $c_1y_1+c_2y_2\equiv0$ implies $c_1=c_2=0$.
- Two linearly independent solutions of a homogeneous second-order linear ODE form a **basis** (fundamental system) of solutions.
- The general homogeneous solution is $y_h=c_1y_1+c_2y_2$.

## 1.4. Wronskian (Sec. 2.6)

- The Wronskian of two differentiable functions is

$$
W(y_1,y_2)(x)=\begin{vmatrix}y_1&y_2\\y_1'&y_2'\end{vmatrix}=y_1y_2'-y_2y_1'.
$$

## 1.5. Characteristic equation (Sec. 2.2)

- For $y''+ay'+by=0$ with constant coefficients, substituting $y=e^{\lambda x}$ gives the characteristic equation $\lambda^2+a\lambda+b=0$.

## 1.6. Differential operator (Sec. 2.3)

- The derivative operator is $Dy=y'$ and the identity operator is $Iy=y$.
- A linear differential operator satisfies $L(c_1u+c_2v)=c_1Lu+c_2Lv$.
- A constant-coefficient equation can be written $(D^2+aD+bI)y=r(x)$.

## 1.7. Euler–Cauchy equation (Sec. 2.5)

- A homogeneous Euler–Cauchy equation has the form $x^2y''+axy'+by=0$ with constant $a,b$; its coefficients depend on powers of $x$.

## 1.8. Complementary and particular solutions (Sec. 2.7)

- For a nonhomogeneous equation, $y_h$ is a general solution of the corresponding homogeneous equation, and $y_p$ is any one solution of the nonhomogeneous equation.
- The complete general solution is $y=y_h+y_p$.

## 1.9. Natural frequency, damping, and resonance (Secs. 2.4, 2.8)

- For a mass–spring system $my''+cy'+ky=0$, $m$ is mass, $c$ the damping constant, and $k$ the spring constant.
- The undamped natural angular frequency is $\omega_0=\sqrt{k/m}$.
- **Resonance** refers to the excitation of large oscillations when forcing and natural frequencies coincide (undamped case); damping can yield a finite resonant peak.

## 1.10. Transient and steady-state responses (Secs. 2.8, 2.9)

- In a damped forced linear system, the decaying homogeneous response is the **transient response**; the persistent periodic particular response is the **steady-state response**.

# 2. Theorems

## 2.1. Fundamental theorem: superposition (Sec. 2.1, Theorem 1)

If $y_1,y_2$ solve $y''+py'+qy=0$ on $I$, then for arbitrary constants $c_1,c_2$,

$$
y=c_1y_1+c_2y_2
$$

also solves the same homogeneous equation on $I$. This does not generally hold for nonhomogeneous or nonlinear equations.

## 2.2. Existence and uniqueness (Sec. 2.6, Theorem 1)

If $p(x),q(x),r(x)$ are continuous on an open interval $I$ containing $x_0$, then

$$
y''+p(x)y'+q(x)y=r(x),\quad y(x_0)=K_0,\quad y'(x_0)=K_1
$$

has exactly one solution on $I$.

## 2.3. Wronskian criterion (Sec. 2.6, Theorem 2)

Let $p,q$ be continuous on an open interval $I$, and let $y_1,y_2$ solve $y''+py'+qy=0$ on $I$. Then:

- $y_1,y_2$ are linearly dependent on $I$ if and only if $W(y_1,y_2)(x_0)=0$ at some $x_0\in I$.
- If the Wronskian vanishes at one point, it vanishes throughout $I$.
- If it is nonzero at one point, it is nonzero throughout $I$, and the solutions are independent.

## 2.4. Existence of a general homogeneous solution (Sec. 2.6, Theorem 3)

If $p,q$ are continuous on an open interval $I$, the homogeneous equation has two linearly independent solutions $y_1,y_2$ on $I$, hence a general solution $y=c_1y_1+c_2y_2$.

## 2.5. General solution includes every solution (Sec. 2.6, Theorem 4)

Under the same continuity assumptions, every solution of the homogeneous equation on $I$ is $c_1y_1+c_2y_2$ for a basis $y_1,y_2$. Therefore the equation has no singular solutions.

## 2.6. Nonhomogeneous solution structure (Sec. 2.7)

For a linear equation $Ly=r$, if $Ly_p=r$ and $Ly_h=0$, then $L(y_h+y_p)=r$. Conversely, the difference of any two solutions of $Ly=r$ solves $Ly=0$. Thus every solution is $y_h+y_p$.

# 3. Solution Methods and Fundamental Formulas

## 3.1. Reduction of order (Sec. 2.1)

Given a nonzero solution $y_1$ of $y''+py'+qy=0$ on an interval, a second solution is obtained from $y_2=uy_1$:

$$
y_2(x)=y_1(x)\int\frac{e^{-\int p(x)\,dx}}{y_1(x)^2}\,dx.
$$

Apply on an interval where $y_1\ne0$.

## 3.2. Constant coefficients: distinct real roots (Sec. 2.2)

For $y''+ay'+by=0$, if $\lambda_1\ne\lambda_2$ are real roots of $\lambda^2+a\lambda+b=0$,

$$
y=c_1e^{\lambda_1x}+c_2e^{\lambda_2x}.
$$

## 3.3. Constant coefficients: repeated real root (Sec. 2.2)

For a double root $\lambda$,

$$
y=(c_1+c_2x)e^{\lambda x}.
$$

## 3.4. Constant coefficients: complex roots (Sec. 2.2)

For roots $\lambda=\alpha\pm i\beta$ with $\beta\ne0$,

$$
y=e^{\alpha x}(c_1\cos\beta x+c_2\sin\beta x).
$$

## 3.5. Euler–Cauchy equation (Sec. 2.5)

For $x^2y''+axy'+by=0$ on $x>0$, try $y=x^m$. The auxiliary equation is

$$
m(m-1)+am+b=0.
$$

- Distinct real roots $m_1,m_2$: $y=c_1x^{m_1}+c_2x^{m_2}$.
- Repeated real root $m$: $y=x^m(c_1+c_2\ln x)$.
- Complex roots $m=\alpha\pm i\beta$: $y=x^\alpha[c_1\cos(\beta\ln x)+c_2\sin(\beta\ln x)]$.

For $x<0$, use corresponding formulas involving $|x|$ on an interval avoiding zero.

## 3.6. Method of undetermined coefficients (Sec. 2.7)

For a constant-coefficient nonhomogeneous equation, choose a trial particular solution of the same general type as the forcing term: polynomial, exponential, sine/cosine, or their suitable products and sums. Substitute into the ODE and determine the unknown coefficients.

**Modification rule:** If a trial term overlaps a homogeneous solution, multiply the relevant trial expression by a sufficient power of $x$ to obtain a linearly independent form.

## 3.7. Variation of parameters (Sec. 2.10)

For $y''+p(x)y'+q(x)y=r(x)$, let $y_1,y_2$ be a basis of the homogeneous equation, with Wronskian $W\ne0$. A particular solution is

$$
y_p=-y_1\int\frac{y_2r}{W}\,dx+y_2\int\frac{y_1r}{W}\,dx.
$$

This formula requires the equation in standard form, with leading coefficient $1$.

# 4. Applications and Connections

## 4.1. Free mass–spring oscillations (Sec. 2.4)

Newton's law gives

$$
my''+cy'+ky=0,\qquad m>0,\quad k>0,\quad c\ge0.
$$

- Undamped ($c=0$): $y=A\cos\omega_0t+B\sin\omega_0t$, with $\omega_0=\sqrt{k/m}$ and period $T=2\pi/\omega_0$.
- Overdamped ($c^2>4mk$): two distinct negative real characteristic roots; nonoscillatory decay.
- Critically damped ($c^2=4mk$): repeated negative root; $y=(A+Bt)e^{-ct/(2m)}$.
- Underdamped ($0<c^2<4mk$): $y=e^{-ct/(2m)}(A\cos\omega_dt+B\sin\omega_dt)$, where

$$
\omega_d=\sqrt{\frac{k}{m}-\frac{c^2}{4m^2}}.
$$

## 4.2. Forced oscillations and resonance (Sec. 2.8)

For sinusoidal forcing,

$$
my''+cy'+ky=F_0\cos\omega t.
$$

When $c>0$, the steady-state response can be written $y_p=C^*\cos(\omega t-\eta)$, with

$$
C^*(\omega)=\frac{F_0}{\sqrt{(k-m\omega^2)^2+c^2\omega^2}}.
$$

The phase lag is determined by the sine and cosine coefficients (using the appropriate quadrant).

For $c=0$ and $\omega=\omega_0$, the resonant particular solution is

$$
y_p(t)=\frac{F_0}{2m\omega_0}\,t\sin(\omega_0t).
$$

For $c>0$, a positive-frequency maximum of $C^*$ occurs when $c^2<2mk$, at

$$
\omega_{\max}=\sqrt{\frac{k}{m}-\frac{c^2}{2m^2}}.
$$

## 4.3. RLC electric circuit (Sec. 2.9)

Kirchhoff's voltage law for a series RLC circuit gives, with charge $Q$ and current $I=Q'$,

$$
LQ''+RQ'+\frac1C Q=E(t).
$$

Differentiating the current form yields

$$
LI''+RI'+\frac1C I=E'(t).
$$

For sinusoidal voltage $E(t)=E_0\sin\omega t$, the reactance is

$$
S=\omega L-\frac{1}{\omega C}.
$$

The steady-state current amplitude is

$$
I_0=\frac{E_0}{\sqrt{R^2+S^2}}.
$$

The electric circuit equation is mathematically analogous to the forced mass–spring equation, with inductance corresponding to mass, resistance to damping, and inverse capacitance to spring stiffness.
