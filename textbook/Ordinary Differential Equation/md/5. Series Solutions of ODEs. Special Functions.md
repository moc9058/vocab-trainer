> **Source:** Kreyszig, *Advanced Engineering Mathematics*, 10th ed., Chapter 5, pp. 167–202 (§§5.1–5.5).

# 1. Definitions

## 1.1. Power series and ordinary point (§5.1)

A power series centered at $x_0$ is $\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}a_m(x-x_0)^m$. The radius of convergence specifies where it converges. For $y''+p(x)y'+q(x)y=0$, $x_0$ is ordinary when $p,q$ are analytic there.

## 1.2. Legendre equation (§5.2)

Legendre's equation is $(1-x^2)y''-2xy'+n(n+1)y=0$. For nonnegative integer $n$, the normalized polynomial solution is $P_n(x)$ with $P_n(1)=1$.

## 1.3. Regular singular point and Frobenius series (§5.3)

The origin is regular singular when $xp(x)$ and $x^2q(x)$ are analytic at zero. A Frobenius solution has the form $y=x^r\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}a_mx^m$, with $a_0\ne0$. The indicial equation determines $r$.

## 1.4. Bessel equation and functions (§§5.4–5.5)

Bessel's equation is $x^2y''+xy'+(x^2-\nu^2)y=0$. The standard solutions are $J_\nu$ (first kind) and $Y_\nu$ (second kind).

## 1.5. Gamma function (§5.4)

For positive real $z$, $\Gamma(z)=\int_0^\infty e^{-t}t^{z-1}\,dt$. It satisfies $\Gamma(z+1)=z\Gamma(z)$ and $\Gamma(n+1)=n!$ for nonnegative integers $n$.

# 2. Theorems

## 2.1. Existence of power series solutions (§5.1, Theorem 1)

If the coefficients p(x), q(x) are analytic at an ordinary point, the second-order homogeneous equation has two independent analytic solutions there.

## 2.2. Frobenius method (§5.3, Theorem 1)

At a regular singular point, at least one Frobenius-type series solution exists. Its leading exponent is a root of the indicial equation.

## 2.3. Three Frobenius cases (§5.3, Theorem 2)

If the indicial roots do not differ by an integer, two Frobenius series are available. Equal roots, or roots differing by a positive integer, may require logarithmic terms in a second solution.

## 2.4. Bessel recurrences (§5.4, Theorem 1)

Bessel functions satisfy adjacent-order recurrence and differentiation identities, including $J_{\nu-1}(x)+J_{\nu+1}(x)=\frac{2\nu}{x}J_\nu(x)$ and $J_{\nu-1}(x)-J_{\nu+1}(x)=2J'_\nu(x)$.

# 3. Solution Methods and Fundamental Formulas

## 3.1. Power-series method (§5.1)

Set $y=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}a_m(x-x_0)^m$. Substitute into the ODE, align exponents, equate coefficients, and derive the recurrence.

## 3.2. Legendre polynomials (§5.2)

$$
a_{m+2}=\frac{m(m+1)-n(n+1)}{(m+2)(m+1)}a_m.
$$

For nonnegative integer $n$, a series terminates. Rodrigues' formula is

$$
P_n(x)=\frac{1}{2^nn!}\frac{d^n}{dx^n}(x^2-1)^n.
$$

## 3.3. Frobenius indicial equation (§5.3)

For $x^2y''+xb(x)y'+c(x)y=0$, substitute $y=x^r\sum a_mx^m$. The indicial equation is

$$
r(r-1)+b(0)r+c(0)=0.
$$

Find the recurrence for each admissible root; a second solution may require a logarithmic term.

## 3.4. Bessel functions (§§5.4–5.5)

For $\nu\ge0$, the first-kind Bessel function has series

$$
J_\nu(x)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^m}{m!\Gamma(m+\nu+1)}\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)^{2m+\nu}.
$$

For $x>0$, a general solution of Bessel's equation is $y=c_1J_\nu(x)+c_2Y_\nu(x)$. For integer $n$, $J_{-n}=(-1)^nJ_n$, so $J_{-n}$ is not independent.

# 4. Applications and Connections

- Legendre polynomials occur in spherical-coordinate problems and orthogonal expansions.
- Bessel functions arise in cylindrical and radial boundary-value problems.
- The ordinary-point and regular-singular-point criteria determine the appropriate series method.
