# Ordinary Differential Equations: engineering mathematics assessment reference

## Assumed knowledge and boundaries

Assume standard first-year university calculus: limits, derivatives, chain and product rules, elementary antiderivatives, substitution, integration by parts, definite and simple improper integrals, and introductory Taylor series. Elementary algebra, trigonometry, exponential and logarithmic functions are available without introductory explanation. Do not assume prior differential equations or a separate linear algebra course. Eigenpairs, Wronskians, matrices, partial derivatives, special functions and transforms are course content supported by the cited notes; supply auxiliary computation rules where the summaries are insufficient. Advanced real or complex analysis, PDE theory, distribution theory, spectral expansions and abstract stability theory are not external prerequisites.

These English notes are structured paraphrases of Kreyszig, Advanced Engineering Mathematics, tenth edition, Chapters 1–6, imported from Notion. They omit worked examples and exercise sets and are not full textbook transcriptions. Assess only what the selected objective and source support. A theorem statement permits application, not a proof requiring omitted theory. Earlier objectives provide prerequisite techniques; later chapters cannot become implicit prerequisites. The mathematical profile serves this engineering mathematics course, including physical applications.

## Exercise design

Frequently choose physical models and substantive mathematical problems rather than isolated formula recall. Use cooling and growth, damped and forced vibrations, electric circuits, coupled systems, pendulum equilibria, radial ODEs, switched inputs and impulse responses when supported by the assigned objective. Within an eligible modeling objective, prefer a physical setting; do not turn an assigned proof or series objective into an unrelated application. This is a qualitative authoring preference, not a guaranteed numerical sampling ratio.

Supply physical laws, diagrams expressed as equations, parameters and constitutive assumptions that are absent from the notes. Do not test unstated physics knowledge. State units, sign conventions, forcing, initial or boundary data and the interval of interest. Require a concrete solution, derived expression, justified determination or proof. Include interpretation only alongside mathematical evidence. Preserve physical admissibility, equilibria lost by division, coefficient continuity, singular points, resonance multiplicities and the limits of approximations. Accept equivalent exact symbolic answers; state tolerances when numerical values are required.

Easy exercises supply a method and limited steps. Standard exercises require method selection and application. Hard exercises require strategy, parameter cases or assumption analysis rather than long arithmetic. Do not infer nonlinear stability from an exceptional Jacobian or impose a missing special-function identity. Radial models may supply the ODE directly; they do not authorize a PDE derivation.

## Representative scoped exercises

The following are design anchors, not extra source facts or mandatory templates. Each remains subordinate to the assigned objective and selected chapter. Expected evidence is for generation and verification, not text to reveal with the problem.

- Chapter 1, `ode-growth-cooling`, derive, standard: A body cools in ambient temperature 20 degrees Celsius with initial temperature 80 degrees and temperature 50 after 10 minutes. Under Newton cooling, derive its temperature and the time to reach 30 degrees. Expected: $k=\log 2/10$ per minute, $T(t)=20+60e^{-kt}$ and $t=10\log 6/\log 2$ minutes; justify the constant ambient model and threshold.
- Chapter 1, `ode-exact`, determine, standard: Determine exactness of $(2xy+1)\,dx+x^2\,dy=0$ on the plane and obtain its solution family. Expected: both cross partials equal $2x$, potential $x^2y+x=C$, with any local explicit representation restricted appropriately.
- Chapter 2, `ode-oscillations`, compute, standard: With coherent SI units, solve $2y''+4y'+10y=0$, $y(0)=1$, $y'(0)=0$, and determine the damping type. Expected: $y=e^{-t}(\cos 2t+\tfrac12\sin 2t)$, underdamped; roots, units and data checked.
- Chapter 2, `ode-basis`, prove, standard: For a linear operator $L$, prove that the difference of two solutions of $Ly=r$ solves the homogeneous equation, and derive the complementary-plus-particular representation when a homogeneous basis is supplied. Expected: explicit use of linearity and basis spanning, without a proof of an omitted existence theorem.
- Chapter 3, `ode-higher-roots`, derive, standard: Find the real general solution of $(D-1)^2(D^2+4)y=0$. Expected: $(c_1+c_2x)e^x+c_3\cos 2x+c_4\sin 2x$, four independent modes and a justified multiplicity argument.
- Chapter 4, `ode-nonlinear-model`, determine, hard: Given $\theta'=v$, $v'=-(g/L)\sin\theta$ with $g,L>0$, find equilibria and determine which local conclusions follow from the source linearization criteria. Expected: $(n\pi,0)$; odd multiples have a saddle; even multiples have imaginary linear eigenvalues, so the stated nonlinear criterion is inconclusive. Do not infer nonlinear centers solely from the Jacobian or demand external energy theory.
- Chapter 5, `ode-power-series`, derive, standard: For $y''+xy=0$, $y(0)=1$, $y'(0)=0$, derive the recurrence and terms through degree six. Expected: $a_2=0$, $(m+2)(m+1)a_{m+2}+a_{m-1}=0$ for $m\ge1$, giving $1-x^3/6+x^6/180$ through that degree; verify indexing and the ordinary point.
- Chapter 5, `ode-bessel`, determine, standard: Supply the radial equation $r^2R''+rR'+\lambda^2r^2R=0$ for $r>0$, $\lambda>0$. Determine its Bessel order and give a general solution. Expected: order zero and $A J_0(\lambda r)+B Y_0(\lambda r)$ by substitution. Do not demand boundedness at zero without supplying the needed limiting behavior.
- Chapter 6, `ode-switched-impulse`, compute, standard: Solve $y''+y=\delta(t-\pi)$ with zero initial state using Laplace transforms. Expected: $Y=e^{-\pi s}/(s^2+1)$ and $y=u(t-\pi)\sin(t-\pi)$; displacement continuous and velocity jump one in the normalized impulse model.
- Chapter 6, `ode-convolution`, derive, standard: Solve $y(t)=1+\int_0^t y(\tau)\,d\tau$ for causal $y$. Expected: $Y=1/s+Y/s=1/(s-1)$ on a valid half-plane and $y=e^t$; substitute to check the integral equation.
