> Throughout this chapter, $F=\mathbb R$ or $\mathbb C$. The zero polynomial has degree $-\infty$. Zeros are distinct unless multiplicity is explicitly stated.

# 1. Definitions

## 1.1. A number $\lambda\in F$ is called a <b>zero</b> or <b>root</b> of a polynomial $p \in \mathcal{P}(F)$ if $p(\lambda)=0$.

# 2. Theorems

## 2.1. Zeros of Polynomials

### 2.1.1. Suppose $m$ is a positive integer and $p\in\mathcal{P}_m(F)$ . Suppose $\lambda\in F$. Then $p(\lambda)=0$ if and only if there exists a polynomial $q\in\mathcal{P}_{m-1}(F)$ such that

$$
p(z)=(z-\lambda)q(z),\ \forall z\in F.
$$

### 2.1.2. Suppose $m$ is a positive integer and $0\ne p\in\mathcal{P}_{m}(F)$. Then $p$ has at most $m$ distinct zeros in $F$.

### 2.1.3. Suppose that $p,s\in\mathcal{P}(F)$, with $s \neq 0$. Then there exist unique polynomials $q,r\in\mathcal{P}(F)$ such that

$$
p=sq+r
$$

where $r=0$ or $\deg r<\deg s$.

Proof sketch. If $n=\deg p\geq m=\deg s$, the list $1,z,\ldots,z^{m-1},s,zs,\ldots,z^{n-m}s$ is a basis of $\mathcal P_n(F)$. Expanding $p$ in this basis gives $p=sq+r$; uniqueness follows from the degree bound. If $\deg p<\deg s$ (including $p=0$), take $q=0$ and $r=p$.

### 2.1.4. Every nonconstant polynomial with complex coefficients has a zero in $\mathbb{C}$.

Proof sketch. Because $|p(z)|\to\infty$ as $|z|\to\infty$, $|p|$ attains a minimum at some $z_0$. If $p(z_0)\ne0$, write $p(z_0+w)/p(z_0)=1+aw^k+O(w^{k+1})$ with $a\ne0$ and choose the argument of a small $w$ so that $aw^k$ is negative real. Then $|p(z_0+w)|<|p(z_0)|$, a contradiction. Complex $k$th roots used in this choice follow from De Moivre’s formula:

$$
(\cos\theta + i\sin\theta)^k=\cos k\theta + i\sin k\theta,\qquad k\in\mathbb N.
$$

### 2.1.5. If $p\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{C})$ is a nonconstant polynomial, then $p$ has a unique factorization (except for the order of the factors) of the form

$$
p(z)=c(z-\lambda_1)\cdots(z-\lambda_m),
$$

where $m=\deg p\geq1$, $c\ne0$ is the leading coefficient, and the roots $\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_m\in\mathbb C$ are listed with multiplicity.

### 2.1.6. Suppose $p\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{C})$ is a polynomial with real coefficients and $p(\lambda)=0$. Then $\overline{\lambda}$ is also a zero of $p$.
