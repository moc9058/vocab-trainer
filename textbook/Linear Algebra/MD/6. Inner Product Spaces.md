# 6. Inner Product Spaces

Last edited in Notion: 2026-10-27T14:54:51.857Z

> Throughout this chapter, $F=\mathbb R$ or $\mathbb C$, and inner products are linear in the first variable and conjugate-linear in the second. Every statement involving $T^\dagger$ assumes that $V,W$ are finite-dimensional inner product spaces. For general inner product spaces, statements about projection and double orthogonal complements here assume that $U$ is finite-dimensional.

# 1. Definitions

## 1.1. Inner Products and Norms

### 1.1.1. <span style="color: red">(Inner product)</span> An inner product on $V$ is a function that takes each ordered pair $(u,v)$ of elements of $V$ to a number $\langle u,v\rangle\in F$ and has the following properties.

(1) $\langle v,v\rangle\in\mathbb R$ and $\langle v,v \rangle \geq 0$

(2) $\langle v,v\rangle=0$ if and only if $v=0$

(3) $\langle u+v,w \rangle=\langle u,w\rangle + \langle v,w\rangle$

(4) $\langle \lambda v,w \rangle=\lambda \langle v,w\rangle$

(5) $\langle w,v \rangle=\overline{\langle v,w \rangle}$

- Note that an <b>inner product space</b> is a vector space along with an inner product on $V$.
- Note that for each fixed $v\in V$, $u\mapsto\langle u, v\rangle$ is a linear map from $V$ to $F$.

### 1.1.2. <span style="color: red">(Norm)</span> For $v\in V$, the norm of $v$ is defined by

$$
\|v\|=\sqrt{\langle v,v \rangle}.
$$

### 1.1.3. Two vectors $v,w \in V$ are called <b>orthogonal</b> if $\langle v,w \rangle=0$.

## 1.2. Orthonormal Bases

### 1.2.1. A list of vectors $\beta=(e_1,\ldots,e_n)$ is called <b>orthonormal</b> if

$$
\langle e_i, e_j \rangle=\delta_{i,j}, \|e_i\|=1.
$$

## 1.3. Orthogonal Complements and Minimization Problems

### 1.3.1. If $U$ is a subset of $V$, then the orthogonal complement of $U$ is denoted by $U^{\perp}$, defined as follows.

$$
U^{\perp}=\{v\in V:\langle u,v \rangle = 0\text{ for every }u\in U\}.
$$

- Note that $G\subseteq H \rightarrow H^{\perp}\subseteq G^{\perp}$.

### 1.3.2. Let $V$ be an inner product space and let $U$ be a finite-dimensional subspace of $V$. Then the orthogonal projection $P_U\in\mathcal{L}(V)$ is defined as follows.

$$
P_U(v)=u,\ v=u+u^{\perp}
$$

- Here $v=u+u^{\perp}$ is the unique decomposition with $u\in U$ and $u^{\perp}\in U^{\perp}$. For infinite-dimensional Hilbert spaces, the same decomposition holds for closed subspaces. Closedness alone does not suffice in an arbitrary incomplete inner product space.

### 1.3.3. <span style="color: red">(Pseudoinverse)</span> Suppose $V,W$ are finite-dimensional inner product spaces and $T\in\mathcal{L}(V,W)$. The <b>pseudoinverse</b> $T^{\dagger}\in\mathcal{L}(W,V)$ of $T$ is a linear map defined by

$$
T^{\dagger}w=\big(T|_{(\ker(T))^{\perp}}\big)^{-1}P_{\operatorname{range}(T)}w
$$

- The restriction $T|_{(\ker T)^\perp}:(\ker T)^\perp\to\operatorname{range}T$ is an isomorphism under the finite-dimensional assumptions.

# 2. Theorems

## 2.1. Inner Products and Norms

### 2.1.1. (Cauchy-Schwarz inequality) Suppose $u,v\in V$. Then

$$
|\langle u,v \rangle| \leq \|u\|\|v\|.
$$

This inequality is an equality if and only if one of $u,v$ is a scalar multiple of the other.

- For $v\ne0$, note that

$$
u=\cfrac{\langle u,v \rangle}{\|v\|^2}v+\bigg(u-\cfrac{\langle u,v \rangle}{\|v\|^2}v\bigg)
$$

### 2.1.2. <span style="color: red">(Triangle inequality)</span> Suppose $u,v\in V$. Then

$$
\|u+v\|\leq \|u\|+\|v\|
$$

### 2.1.3. <span style="color: red">(Parallelogram equality)</span> Suppose $u,v\in V$. Then

$$
\|u+v\|^2+\|u-v\|^2=2(\|u\|^2+\|v\|^2)
$$

## 2.2. Orthonormal Bases

### 2.2.1. Every orthonormal set is linearly independent.

Proof sketch. Suppose $e_1,\ldots,e_n$ is an orthonormal list of vectors of $V$. Then

$$
\|a_1e_1+\cdots a_ne_n\|^2=|a_1|^2+\cdots |a_n|^2.
$$

### 2.2.2. <span style="color: red">(Bessel’s inequality)</span> Suppose $e_1,\ldots,e_n$ is an orthonormal list of vectors in $V$. If $v\in V$, then

$$
|\langle v,e_1 \rangle|^2+\cdots |\langle v,e_n \rangle|^2 \leq \|v\|^2
$$

### 2.2.3. Suppose $e_1,\ldots,e_n$ is an orthonormal basis of $V$ and $u,v\in V$. Then

(1) $v=\langle v,e_1 \rangle e_1+\cdots\langle v, e_n \rangle e_n$

(2) $\|v\|^2=|\langle v,e_1 \rangle|^2 + \cdots |\langle v,e_n \rangle|^2$

(3) $\langle u,v \rangle=\langle u,e_1\rangle \overline{\langle v,e_1 \rangle}+\cdots\langle u,e_n \rangle\overline{\langle v,e_n \rangle}$.

### 2.2.4. <span style="color: red">(Gram-Schmidt Procedure)</span> Suppose $v_1,\ldots,v_m$ is a list of linearly independent vectors in $V$. Let $f_1=v_1$. For $k=2,\ldots,m$, define

$$
f_k=v_k-\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\cfrac{\langle v_k,f_i \rangle}{\|f_i\|^2}f_i.
$$

Let $e_i=\cfrac{f_i}{\|f_i\|}$. Then $e_1,\ldots,e_m$ is an orthonormal list of vectors in $V$ such that

$$
\operatorname{span}\{e_1,\ldots,e_k\}=\operatorname{span}\{v_1,\ldots,v_k\}
$$

for each $k=1,\ldots,m$.

- Note that every finite-dimensional inner product space has an orthonormal basis.
- Note that every orthonormal list in a finite-dimensional inner product space can be extended to an orthonormal basis.

### 2.2.5. Suppose $V$ is finite-dimensional inner product space and $T\in \mathcal{L}(V)$. Then $T$ has an upper-triangular matrix representation with respect to some orthonormal basis if and only if the minimal polynomial of $T$ is in the form of $(z-\lambda_1)\cdots(z-\lambda_m)$.

- Note that the Gram-Schmidt process assures $\operatorname{span}\{v_1,\ldots,v_k\}=\operatorname{span}\{e_1,\ldots,e_k\}$ for all $k=1,\ldots,m$.

<span style="color: red">(Schur’s theorem)</span> Every operator in a finite-dimensional complex inner product space has an orthonormal basis with respect to which the matrix representation is upper-triangular.

### 2.2.6. <span style="color: red">(Riesz representation theorem)</span> Suppose $V$ is finite-dimensional inner product space and $\varphi$ is a linear functional on $V$. Then there exists a unique vector $v\in V$ such that

$$
\varphi(x)=\langle x,v\rangle
$$

for all $x\in V$.

- Note that $v=\overline{\varphi(e_1)}e_1+\cdots +\overline{\varphi(e_n)}e_n$ and it is unique regardless of which orthonormal $e_1,\ldots,e_n$ is chosen.

## 2.3. Orthogonal Complements and Minimization Problems

### 2.3.1. Suppose $V$ is an inner product space and let $U$ be a finite-dimensional subspace of $V$. Then

$$
V=U\oplus U^{\perp}.
$$

$\because\ v=\underbrace{\big(\langle v,u_1\rangle u_1+\cdots +\langle v,u_n \rangle u_n\big)}_{u}+\underbrace{\big(v-\langle v,u_1\rangle u_1-\cdots -\langle v,u_n \rangle u_n\big)}_{w}$

- Note that if $V$ is finite-dimensional, then $\dim(V)=\dim(U)+\dim(U^{\perp}).$

### 2.3.2. Suppose $V$ is an inner product space and let $U$ be a finite-dimensional subspace of $V$.  Then

$$
\big(U^{\perp}\big)^{\perp}=U.
$$

- Note that $U^{\perp}=\{0\} \iff U=V$.

### 2.3.3. Suppose $U$ is a finite-dimensional subspace. Then $P_U$ has following properties.

(1) $\operatorname{range}(P_U)=U$

(2) $\ker(P_U)=U^{\perp}$

(3) $v-P_Uv \in U^{\perp}$ for every $v\in V$

(4) $P_U^2=P_U$

(5) $\|P_Uv\|^2\leq\|v\|^2$ for every $v \in V$

(6) Suppose $e_1,\ldots,e_n$ is an orthonormal basis of $U$. Then

$$
P_Uv=\langle v,e_1 \rangle e_1+\cdots \langle v,e_n \rangle e_n.
$$

### 2.3.4. Suppose $V$ is finite-dimensional. For each $v\in V$, define $\varphi_v\in V'$ by

$$
\varphi_v(u)=\langle u,v \rangle
$$

for each $u \in V$. Then $v \mapsto \varphi_v$ is a bijection from $V$ to $V'$.

- Note that the mapping $v \mapsto \varphi_v$ is not a linear map if $F=\mathbb{C}$.

### 2.3.5. Suppose $U$ is a finite-dimensional subspace of $V$, $v\in V$ and $u \in U$. Then

$$
\|v-P_Uv\|\leq\|v-u\|
$$

and the inequality becomes equality if and only if $u=P_Uv$.

### 2.3.6. Let $T\in \mathcal{L}(V,W)$ between finite-dimensional inner product spaces. Then

(1) If $T$ is invertible, then $T^{-1}=T^{\dagger}$.

(2) $TT^{\dagger}=P_{\operatorname{range}(T)}$

(3) $T^{\dagger}T=P_{(\ker(T))^{\perp}}$

- All pseudoinverse assertions here use finite-dimensional inner product spaces; infinite-dimensional extensions require additional hypotheses.

### 2.3.7. Let $T\in\mathcal{L}(V,W)$ between finite-dimensional inner product spaces and $w\in W$. Then

(1) If $v\in V$, then

$$
\|T(T^{\dagger}w)-w\|\leq\|Tv-w\|
$$

with equality if and only if $v\in T^{\dagger}w+\ker(T)$.

(2) If $v\in T^{\dagger}w+\ker(T)$, then

$$
\|T^{\dagger}w\|\leq\|v\|
$$

with equality if and only if $v=T^{\dagger}w$.

### 2.3.8. Let $T \in \mathcal{L}(V,W)$ between finite-dimensional inner product spaces. Then

(1) $TT^{\dagger}T=T$ and $T^{\dagger}TT^{\dagger}=T^{\dagger}$

(2) $\big(T^{\dagger}\big)^{\dagger}=T$
