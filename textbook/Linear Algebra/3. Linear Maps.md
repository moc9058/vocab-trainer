# 3. Linear Maps

Source: https://www.notion.so/40669428c86181c294d8e34d78697d2e

> Throughout this chapter, $F=\mathbb R$ or $\mathbb C$, and all vector spaces use $F$. Bases used for matrices are ordered. In $[T]_{\mathscr B}^{\mathscr C}$, $\mathscr B$ is the domain basis and $\mathscr C$ is the codomain basis; $[T]_{\mathscr B}=[T]_{\mathscr B}^{\mathscr B}$. When $\mathcal M(T)$ is written without bases, fixed compatible ordered bases are understood.

## 1. Definitions

### 1.1. Linear maps and their algebraic operations

A map $T:V\to W$ is linear if $T(au+bv)=aTu+bTv$ for all $u,v\in V$ and $a,b\in F$. The space of all such maps is denoted by $\mathcal L(V,W)$.

For $S,T \in \mathcal{L}(V,W)$, $(S+T)(v)=Sv+Tv$, $(\lambda T)(v)=\lambda (Tv)$. For composable maps $T\in\mathcal L(U,V)$ and $S\in\mathcal L(V,W)$, $(ST)(u)=S(Tu)$.

Composition is associative, has identity maps, and distributes over addition whenever the domains and codomains are compatible.

### 1.2. Kernel, range, nullity, and rank

For $T\in\mathcal L(V,W)$, $\ker T=\{v\in V:Tv=0\}$ and $\operatorname{range}T=T(V)$. Define $\operatorname{nullity}T=\dim\ker T$ and $\operatorname{rank}T=\dim\operatorname{range}T$ when these dimensions are finite.

### 1.3. Let $V,W$ be finite-dimensional vector spaces with bases $\mathscr{B}=\{v_1,\ldots,v_n\}, \mathscr{C}=\{w_1,\ldots,w_m\}$, respectively. The matrix representation of a linear map $T\in \mathcal{L}(V,W)$ is defined as follows:

$$
\mathcal{M}(T,\mathscr{B},\mathscr{C})=\begin{bmatrix}
T
\end{bmatrix}_{\mathscr{B}}^{\mathscr{C}}=\begin{pmatrix}
A_{1,1} & A_{1,2} & \ldots & A_{1,n}\\
&&\vdots\\
A_{m,1} & A_{m,2} & \ldots & A_{m,n}
\end{pmatrix}
$$

where

$$
Tv_k=\sum_{i=1}^{m}A_{i,k}w_i,\\
\begin{bmatrix}Tv_k\end{bmatrix}_{\mathscr{C}}=\begin{pmatrix}A_{1,k}\\\vdots\\A_{m,k}\end{pmatrix}
$$

for basis $v_1,\ldots,v_n$ of $V$.

### 1.4. Let $m,n$ be positive integers. Then $F^{m\times n}$ is the set of all $m$-by-$n$ matrices with entries in $F$.

### 1.5. $T\in\mathcal{L}(V,W)$ is an isomorphism if it is an invertible linear map.

The spaces $V,W$ are isomorphic if an isomorphism $V\to W$ exists.

### **1.6**. <span style="color: red">(Quotient Space)</span> Suppose $U$ is a subspace of $V$.  Then $V/U=\{v+U:v\in V\}$ is the set of all translates of $U$.

(1) Note that $v+U=\{v+u:u\in U\}$ is called a translate of $U$.

(2) The addition and scalar multiplication are defined on $V/U$ as follows:

$$
(v+U)+(w+U):=(v+w)+U\\
\lambda (v+U):=(\lambda v) + U
$$

Therefore, $V/U$ is a vector space.

### 1.7. <span style="color: red">(Quotient Map)</span> Suppose $U$ is a subspace of $V$. Then the linear map

$$
\pi_U: V \rightarrow V/U,\ \pi_U(v)=v+U
$$

is called a quotient map.

### 1.8. Suppose $T \in \mathcal{L}(V,W)$. Define

$$
\widetilde{T}:V/\ker(T) \rightarrow W,\ \widetilde{T}(v+\ker(T))=Tv
$$

- Note that the definition makes sense as $u+\ker(T)=v+\ker(T)$ implies $u-v\in \ker(T)$, therefore $Tu=Tv$.

### 1.9. <span style="color: red">(Linear Functional)</span> Let $V$ be a vector space. Then the dual space $V'=\mathcal{L}(V,F)$ is the vector space of all linear functionals.

### 1.10. <span style="color: red">(Dual Basis)</span> Suppose $\{v_1,\ldots,v_n\}$ is a basis of $V$. Then $\{\varphi_1,\ldots,\varphi_n\}$ is called the dual basis.

$$
\varphi_j\in \mathcal{L}(V,F)\text{ for each } j\in\{1,\ldots,n\},\text{where}\\
\varphi_j(v_k)=\begin{cases}
1 &\text{if }k = j\\
0 &\text{if }k \neq j
\end{cases}
$$

It can be easily shown that the dual basis indeed forms a basis of $V'$.

### 1.11. <span style="color: red">(Dual Map)</span> Suppose $T\in\mathcal{L}(V,W)$. Then the dual map $T'\in\mathcal{L}(W',V')$ is defined for each $\varphi \in W',$

$$
T'(\varphi)=\varphi \circ T.
$$

### 1.12. <span style="color: red">(Annihilator)</span> Suppose $U \subseteq V$. The annihilator of $U$, denoted by $U^0_{V}$ is defined by

$$
U_V^0=\{\varphi \in V':\varphi(u)=0\text{ for all }u\in U\}.
$$

For a subspace $U$ of a finite-dimensional space $V$,

$$
U^0=\{0\} \iff U=V\\
\text{and}\\
U^0=V' \iff U=\{0\}.
$$

## 2. Theorems

### 2.1. Linear Maps and Isomorphisms

#### 2.1.1. Suppose $v_1,\ldots,v_n$ is a basis of $V$ and $w_1,\ldots,w_n \in W$. Then there exists a unique linear map $T:V \rightarrow W$ such that

$$
Tv_i=w_i,i\in \{1,\ldots,n\}
$$

Proof sketch. $T(c_1v_1+\ldots+c_nv_n)=c_1w_1+\ldots+c_nw_n$,

#### 2.1.2. $T\in\mathcal{L}(V,W)$ is injective (one-to-one) if and only if $\ker(T)=\{0\}$.

#### 2.1.3. Suppose $V$ is finite-dimensional and $T \in \mathcal{L}(V,W)$. Then

$$
\dim(V)=\operatorname{nullity}(T)+\operatorname{rank}(T)
$$

#### 2.1.4. Let $V,W$ be finite-dimensional vector spaces.

(1) If $\dim(V)>\dim(W)$, then no linear map from $V$ to $W$ is injective.

(2) If $\dim(V) < \dim(W)$, then no linear map from $V$ to $W$ is surjective.

(3) Suppose $m<n$. Every homogeneous system of $m$ linear equations in $n$ unknowns has a nonzero solution.

(4) Suppose $m>n$. For any fixed coefficient matrix $A\in F^{m\times n}$, there exists $b\in F^m$ for which $Ax=b$ has no solution.

- Consider the following homogeneous system of m linear equations:

$$
A_{1,1}x_1+A_{1,2}x_2+\ldots+A_{1,n}x_n=0,\\
\vdots\\
A_{m,1}x_1+A_{m,2}x_2+\ldots+A_{m,n}x_n=0.
$$

Define $T:F^n \rightarrow F^m$ by

$$
T(x_1,\ldots,x_n)=\bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{n}A_{1,j}x_j,\ldots,\sum_{j=1}^{n}A_{m,j}x_j\bigg).
$$

#### 2.1.5. Suppose $S,T \in \mathcal{L}(V,W)$. Then $\mathcal{M}(S+T)=\mathcal{M}(S)+\mathcal{M}(T)$.

#### 2.1.6. Suppose $S \in \mathcal{L}(V,W)$ and $T \in \mathcal{L}(U,V)$ for finite-dimensional vector spaces $U,V,W$. Then $\mathcal{M}(ST)=\mathcal{M}(S)\mathcal{M}(T)$.

#### 2.1.7. Suppose $A \in F^{m\times n}, b\in F^{n}$. Then

$$
Ab=\begin{pmatrix}A_{1,1}\\\vdots\\A_{m,1}\end{pmatrix}b_1+\ldots+\begin{pmatrix}A_{1,n}\\\vdots\\A_{m,n}\end{pmatrix}b_n
$$

#### 2.1.8. Suppose $A$ is an $m$-by-$n$ matrix with entries in $F$ and column rank $c\geq1$. Then there exists $C\in F^{m\times c}, R\in F^{c\times n}$ such that $A=CR$.

#### 2.1.9. Suppose $A\in F^{m\times n}$. Then the column rank and row rank of $A$ are the same.

Proof sketch. Consider the following inequalities.

$$
\operatorname{rowrank}(A)\leq\operatorname{colrank}(A)
=\operatorname{rowrank}(A^{\mathsf T})
\leq\operatorname{colrank}(A^{\mathsf T})
=\operatorname{rowrank}(A).
$$

#### 2.1.10. Let $V,W$ be finite-dimensional vector spaces with the same dimension, and let $T\in\mathcal L(V,W)$. Then either injectivity or surjectivity implies invertibility.

#### 2.1.11. Suppose $V,W$ are finite-dimensional vector spaces with $\dim(V)=\dim(W)$ and let $S\in \mathcal{L}(W,V), T\in \mathcal{L}(V,W)$. Then

$$
ST=I\iff TS=I
$$

#### 2.1.12. Two finite-dimensional vector spaces are isomorphic if and only if they have the same dimension.

Note that $\mathcal{M}$ is an isomorphism between $\mathcal{L}(V,W)$ and $F^{m\times n}$.

#### 2.1.13. Let $V,W$ be finite-dimensional vector spaces. Suppose $T \in \mathcal{L}(U,V), S \in \mathcal{L}(V,W)$, where $\mathscr{A}=\{u_1,\ldots,u_p\},\mathscr{B}=\{v_1,\ldots,v_n\},\mathscr{C}=\{w_1,\ldots,w_m\}$ are bases of $U,V,W$, respectively. Then

$$
\begin{bmatrix}
  T(u)
\end{bmatrix}_{\mathscr{B}}
=\begin{bmatrix}
  T
\end{bmatrix}_{\mathscr{A}}^{\mathscr{B}}\begin{bmatrix}u\end{bmatrix}_{\mathscr{A}},\forall u \in U\\
\text{and}\\
\begin{bmatrix}
  ST
\end{bmatrix}_{\mathscr{A}}^{\mathscr{C}}=\begin{bmatrix}
  S
\end{bmatrix}_{\mathscr{B}}^{\mathscr{C}}
\begin{bmatrix}
  T
\end{bmatrix}_{\mathscr{A}}^{\mathscr{B}}
$$

Note that by the second property,

$$
\begin{bmatrix}
  I
\end{bmatrix}_{\mathscr{A}}^{\mathscr{B}}=\big(\begin{bmatrix}
  I
\end{bmatrix}_{\mathscr{B}}^{\mathscr{A}}\big)^{-1}.
$$

#### 2.1.14. Let $V,W$ be finite-dimensional vector spaces and $T\in\mathcal L(V,W)$. Then $\operatorname{rank}(T)=\operatorname{colrank}(\mathcal{M}(T))$.

#### 2.1.15. Let $V, W$ be finite-dimensional vector spaces. Let $\mathscr{A,A'}$ be bases of $V$ and $\mathscr{B,B'}$ be bases of $W$. Then

$$
\begin{bmatrix}
  T
\end{bmatrix}_{\mathscr{A'}}^{\mathscr{B'}}
=\begin{bmatrix}
  I
\end{bmatrix}_{\mathscr{B}}^{\mathscr{B'}}\begin{bmatrix}
  T
\end{bmatrix}_{\mathscr{A}}^{\mathscr{B}}\begin{bmatrix}
  I
\end{bmatrix}_{\mathscr{A'}}^{\mathscr{A}}
$$

In particular, if $V=W$ and $\mathscr{A=B,A'=B'}$, then

$$
\begin{bmatrix}
  T
\end{bmatrix}_{\mathscr{B'}}
=\begin{bmatrix}
  I
\end{bmatrix}_{\mathscr{B}}^{\mathscr{B'}}\begin{bmatrix}
  T
\end{bmatrix}_{\mathscr{B}}\begin{bmatrix}
  I
\end{bmatrix}_{\mathscr{B'}}^{\mathscr{B}}=C^{-1}\begin{bmatrix}
  T
\end{bmatrix}_{\mathscr{B}}C
$$

Here $C=[I]_{\mathscr B'}^{\mathscr B}$ converts new coordinates to old coordinates.

### 2.2. Products and Quotients of Vector Spaces

#### 2.2.1. Let $V_1,\ldots,V_m$ be vector spaces over $F$. Then $V=V_1 \times \ldots \times V_m$ is a vector space over $F$. If $V_i$’s are all finite-dimensional, then $\dim(V)=\dim(V_1)+\ldots+\dim(V_m)$.

#### 2.2.2. Suppose $V_1,\ldots,V_m$ are subspaces of a finite-dimensional vector space $V$. Then $V_1+\ldots+V_m$ is a direct sum if and only if the following $\Gamma$ is injective.

$$
\Gamma : V_1 \times \ldots \times V_m \rightarrow V_1 + \ldots + V_m,\ \Gamma(v_1,\ldots,v_m)=v_1+\ldots+v_m.
$$

Note that direct sum means that every element of $V_1+\cdots+V_m$ can be expressed by the summation of elements of $V_1,\ldots,V_m$ uniquely.

#### 2.2.3. Suppose $U$ is a subspace of $V$ and $v,w \in V$. Then

$$
v-w \in U \iff v+U=w+U \iff (v+U) \cap (w+U) \neq \varnothing.
$$

This theorem indicates that translates of a subspace are either equal or disjoint.

#### 2.2.4. Suppose $V$ is a finite-dimensional vector space and $U$ is a subspace of $V$. Then

$$
\dim(V/U)=\dim(V)-\dim(U)
$$

#### 2.2.5. Suppose $T \in \mathcal{L}(V,W)$. Then

(1) $\widetilde{T}\circ\pi_{\ker(T)}=T$.

(2) $\widetilde{T}$ is injective.

(3) $\operatorname{range}(\widetilde{T})=\operatorname{range}(T)$.

(4) $V/\ker(T)$ and $\operatorname{range}(T)$ are isomorphic vector spaces.

### 2.3. Duality

#### 2.3.1. Let $v_1,\ldots,v_n$ be a basis of $V$ and $\varphi_1,\ldots,\varphi_n$ be the dual basis. Then for each $v\in V$,

$$
v=\varphi_1(v)v_1+\ldots+\varphi_n(v)v_n.
$$

It implies that the dual basis represents the coordinates corresponding to each $v_i$’s.

#### 2.3.2. Suppose $T\in\mathcal{L}(U,V)$. Then

(1) $(S+T)'=S'+T',\ \forall S\in\mathcal{L}(U,V)$

(2) $(\lambda T)'=\lambda T'$

(3) $(ST)'=T'S',\ \forall S\in\mathcal{L}(V,W)$

#### 2.3.3. Suppose $U$ is a subspace of $V$. Then $U_V^0$ is a subspace of $V'$. If $V$ is finite-dimensional, $\dim(U_V^0)+\dim(U)=\dim(V)$.

Proof sketch. Use the inclusion $\iota:U\hookrightarrow V$. Its dual $\iota':V'\to U'$ is surjective by extending a basis of $U$ to a basis of $V$, and $\ker\iota'=U_V^0$. Apply rank–nullity.

#### 2.3.4. Let $T\in\mathcal{L}(V,W)$. Then $\ker(T')=(\operatorname{range}T)^0_W$.

If $V,W$ are finite-dimensional, the following statements hold:

(1) $\operatorname{nullity}(T')=\operatorname{nullity}(T)+\dim(W)-\dim(V)$.

(2) $\operatorname{rank}(T)=\operatorname{rank}(T')$.

(3) $\operatorname{range}(T')=(\ker(T))^0$.

#### 2.3.5. Let $V,W$ be finite-dimensional vector spaces and let $T\in \mathcal{L}(V,W)$. Then

$$
T\text{ is surjective} \iff T'\text{ is injective}\\
\text{and}\\
T\text{ is injective} \iff T'\text{ is surjective}.
$$

Proof sketch. Note that $\operatorname{range}(T)=W \iff [\operatorname{range}(T)]^0=\{0\}$.

#### 2.3.6. Let $V,W$ be finite-dimensional vector spaces with dual spaces $V',W'$, and let $T\in\mathcal L(V,W)$. Let $\mathscr{B}=\{v_1,\ldots,v_n\},\mathscr{B'}=\{\varphi_1,\ldots,\varphi_n\}$ and $\mathscr{C}=\{w_1,\ldots,w_m\},\mathscr{C'}=\{\psi_1,\ldots,\psi_m\}$ be bases and dual bases of $V,W$, respectively. Then

$$
\begin{bmatrix}T'\end{bmatrix}_{\mathscr{C'}}^{\mathscr{B'}}
=\big(\begin{bmatrix}T\end{bmatrix}_{\mathscr{B}}^{\mathscr{C}}\big)^{\mathsf T}.
$$
