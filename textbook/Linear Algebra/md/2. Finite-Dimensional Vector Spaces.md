# 1. Definitions

## 1.1. Span

$$
\operatorname{span}(v_1,\ldots,v_n)=\left\{\sum_{k=1}^na_kv_k:a_1,\ldots,a_n\in F\right\},\\
\operatorname{span}(\varnothing)=\{0\}.
$$

- If $\operatorname{span}(v_1,\ldots,v_n)=V$, we say $\{v_1,\ldots,v_n\}$ spans $V$.

## 1.2. Finite and infinite-dimensionality

- If a vector space $V$ is spanned by a finite number of vectors, $V$ is called finite-dimensional.
- If not finite-dimensional, $V$ is called infinite-dimensional.

## 1.3. Polynomial $\mathcal{P}(F)$

- It is the set of all the polynomials from $F$ to $F$ with coefficients in $F$.
- The zero polynomial is assigned degree $-\infty$ and belongs to every $\mathcal P_m(F)$.
- For a nonnegative integer $m$, $\mathcal{P}_m(F)$ denotes the set of all polynomials with coefficients in $F$ and degree at most $m$.

## 1.4. Linear independence and dependence

- A set of vectors $S$ is linearly independent if for every finite list of distinct vectors $v_1,\ldots,v_k\in S$ and scalars $a_1,\ldots,a_k\in F$, the following is satisfied.

$$
a_1v_1+\ldots+a_kv_k=0 \iff a_1=\ldots=a_k=0
$$

- $\varnothing$ is defined to be linearly independent.
- If a set is not linearly independent, it is called linearly dependent.

## 1.5. Basis

- A basis of $V$ is a set of vectors in $V$ that is linearly independent and spans $V$.

# 2. Theorems

## 2.1. The span of a list is the smallest subspace containing every vector in that list.

## 2.2. Suppose $v_1,\ldots,v_m$ is a linearly dependent list in $V$. Then there exists $k\in\{1,\ldots,m\}$ such that

$$
v_k \in \operatorname{span}(v_1,\ldots,v_{k-1})
$$

Furthermore, if $k$ satisfies the condition above and the $k^{th}$ term is removed from $\{v_1,\ldots,v_m\}$, then the span of the remaining list equals $\operatorname{span}(v_1,\ldots,v_m)$.

## 2.3. In the same vector space, the length of a linearly independent list is at most the length of a finite spanning list.

- As a result of this lemma, every subspace of a finite-dimensional vector space is finite-dimensional.

## 2.4. A list $v_1,\ldots,v_n$ of vectors in $V$ is a basis if and only if every $v \in V$ can be written uniquely in the form

$$
v=a_1v_1+\ldots+a_nv_n\text{ for }a_1,\ldots,a_n\in F
$$

## 2.5. Every finite-dimensional vector space has a basis.

- Note that every finite spanning list of vectors can be reduced to a basis by theorem 2.2.

## 2.6. Every linearly independent set can be extended to a basis of a finite-dimensional vector space.

## 2.7. For every subspace $U$ of a finite-dimensional $V$, there is a subspace $W$ such that $U \oplus W=V$.

## 2.8. The length of any basis of a finite-dimensional vector space $V$ is always the same.

- $\dim(U)\leq \dim(V)$ if $U$ is a subspace of $V$.
- This common length is the dimension $\dim V$, with $\dim\{0\}=0$.
- Every linearly independent list of length $\dim V$ is a basis of $V$.
- Any spanning subset with the length $\dim(V)$ is a basis of $V$.

## 2.9. If $V_1$ and $V_2$ are subspaces of a finite-dimensional vector space, then

$$
\dim(V_1+V_2)=\dim(V_1)+\dim(V_2)-\dim(V_1 \cap V_2)
$$
